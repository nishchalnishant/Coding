# 05. Behavioral and Project Stories

← [Part 33](README.md)

Prepare real examples that show scope, judgment, collaboration, and outcomes. Mid-level expectations often include owning a feature through delivery, handling ambiguity, improving quality or operations, and helping teammates succeed. Do not claim work you did not do.

## Build a story bank

Prepare six to eight stories, each with concise notes:

| Story type | Evidence to include |
|---|---|
| Delivered a complex feature | User need, scope, decisions, collaboration, measurable result |
| Improved reliability or performance | Baseline, diagnosis, fix, validation, sustained impact |
| Handled an incident or failure | Ownership, communication, mitigation, root cause, prevention |
| Disagreed or influenced a decision | Alternatives, evidence, respectful resolution, outcome |
| Worked through ambiguity | Questions, assumptions, milestones, changes as learning arrived |
| Made a mistake or failed | Your responsibility, impact, repair, specific lesson applied later |
| Mentored or unblocked others | Their goal, your approach, outcome and what they owned |
| Balanced delivery and quality | Trade-off, risk control, follow-up work, outcome |

## Answer structure

Use STAR as a memory aid: **Situation** (brief context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (your decisions and collaboration), **Result** (outcome, ideally measurable), then add a short **Learning** when useful. Keep the action section specific. Distinguish your contribution from the team's work.

## Project deep dive

For one recent project, be ready to explain:

- User and business problem; success measure
- Architecture, interfaces, data flow, and your ownership boundary
- Constraints and alternatives considered
- Rollout, testing, monitoring, and operational ownership
- A production issue, design change, or unexpected result
- What you would improve with more time and why

Use numbers only when you can explain their source. If details are confidential, describe the shape of the problem without disclosing protected information.

## Practice questions

- Tell me about a project you owned from problem definition through operation.
- Describe a time you changed your mind after new evidence.
- Tell me about a production issue and how you reduced the chance of recurrence.
- Describe a disagreement about a technical decision.
- What is a piece of work you would do differently now?
- How have you made teammates more effective?

For company-specific behavioral frameworks, map stories to the published values or principles without forcing every story into every category. Re-read the target company's current interview guidance before the loop.
