# Amazon SDE-2 — Behavioral (Leadership Principles)

## Critical context
Amazon uses LPs as a **core filter** — not a formality. SDE-2 candidates need 2–3 strong STAR stories per LP. Every round (including coding) will have LP questions. Weak LP = rejection even with perfect coding.

---

## All 16 Leadership Principles (with SDE-2 focus)

### Tier 1 — Almost Certain to be Asked

#### 1. Customer Obsession
> Leaders start with the customer and work backwards
- SDE-2 angle: How did you advocate for users despite pushback? Did you unblock a customer-impacting bug over a feature?
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you made a decision that wasn't the most technically optimal but was best for the customer."

#### 2. Ownership
> Leaders act on behalf of the entire company, never say "that's not my job"
- SDE-2 angle: You noticed a problem outside your scope and fixed it. You stayed up to resolve an incident you didn't cause.
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you took on something significantly outside your responsibility."

#### 3. Invent and Simplify
> Leaders expect and require innovation, find ways to simplify
- SDE-2 angle: Built a tool to automate a manual process. Simplified a complex system. Created something novel.
- Sample Q: "Describe a time when you found a simple solution to a complex problem."

#### 4. Are Right, A Lot
> Leaders have strong judgment and good instincts
- SDE-2 angle: You disagreed with a technical decision and were right. You made a call under uncertainty.
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time when you had to make an important decision with incomplete data."

#### 5. Deliver Results
> Leaders focus on key inputs and deliver with the right quality and in a timely fashion
- SDE-2 angle: Shipped under pressure. Unblocked the team. Met a deadline despite obstacles.
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you had to deliver under a tight deadline. What did you do?"

#### 6. Dive Deep
> Leaders operate at all levels, stay connected to details
- SDE-2 angle: Dug into root cause of a production bug beyond surface symptoms. Caught something in a code review.
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time when you had to dig deep into data or code to solve a problem."

---

### Tier 2 — High Probability

#### 7. Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
> Leaders respectfully challenge decisions; once decided, commit wholly
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager or team and how you handled it."
- Key: Show you pushed back WITH data, not emotion, and then committed once the decision was made.

#### 8. Bias for Action
> Speed matters. Many decisions are reversible — don't wait for perfect info
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you had to make a quick decision without all the information you needed."
- Key: Show calculated risk-taking, not recklessness.

#### 9. Learn and Be Curious
> Leaders are never done learning; they seek to improve themselves
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you taught yourself something outside your domain to solve a problem."

#### 10. Hire and Develop the Best
> Leaders raise the performance bar with every hire; mentor others
- SDE-2 angle: Mentored a junior engineer. Gave actionable code review feedback. Improved the interview process.
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you helped a colleague grow or improve their skills."

#### 11. Earn Trust
> Leaders listen, speak candidly, treat others respectfully
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you had to build trust with a skeptical stakeholder or teammate."

---

### Tier 3 — Medium (specific teams/roles)

#### 12. Frugality
> Accomplish more with less; constraints breed resourcefulness
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you achieved a goal with limited resources."

#### 13. Think Big
> Thinking small is a self-fulfilling prophecy; bold direction inspires results
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you proposed or worked on something that required a significant change in direction."

#### 14. Insist on the Highest Standards
> Leaders have relentlessly high standards; don't let mediocre work pass
- Sample Q: "Tell me about a time you raised the bar for quality on your team."

#### 15. Strive to be Earth's Best Employer
> Leaders create a safe, productive environment (rarely asked for IC roles)

#### 16. Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility
> Amazon's impact = responsibility to improve (rarely asked at SDE-2)

## STAR Format (strict)

```
Situation: 1–2 sentences. Set the scene. What was the context?
Task:       1 sentence. What was YOUR responsibility specifically?
Action:     3–5 sentences. What YOU did. Use "I", not "we".
Result:     2–3 sentences. Quantify. What was the outcome?
            Did you learn something? What would you do differently?
```

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Saying "we" throughout — interviewers can't assess your contribution | Use "I"; explain your specific role when team was involved |
| Vague results ("it went well", "team was happy") | Quantify: latency reduced by X%, shipped 2 weeks early, blocked 0 incidents |
| Story that's just technical (no interpersonal challenge) | Add the human element — stakeholder conflict, disagreement, mentoring |
| Picking stories that are too old or too junior | Use recent stories that show SDE-2-level scope and impact |
| Not having a failure story ready | Prepare one for "Tell me about a mistake you made" — show self-awareness + fix |

## Prepare These Story Slots

Have a ready story for each:

- [ ] Project I owned end-to-end (shows Ownership + Deliver Results)
- [ ] Time I disagreed and escalated (shows Backbone)
- [ ] Time I fixed something outside my scope (shows Ownership)
- [ ] Hardest technical problem I solved (shows Dive Deep)
- [ ] Time I simplified something complex (shows Invent & Simplify)
- [ ] Time I made a decision with incomplete data (shows Bias for Action + Are Right)
- [ ] Time I mentored or helped someone grow (shows Hire & Develop)
- [ ] Time I failed / made a mistake (shows Learn & Be Curious)
- [ ] Time I dealt with a difficult stakeholder (shows Earn Trust)
- [ ] Most impactful project (shows Think Big + Deliver Results)

## Questions to Ask the Interviewer

These signal ownership and customer focus:
- "What does success look like for someone in this role at the 6-month mark?"
- "What's the biggest technical challenge the team is facing right now?"
- "How does the team balance shipping fast vs. maintaining quality?"
- "What's an example of a decision the team disagreed on and how was it resolved?"
- "How do engineering teams interact with product/customers here?"
